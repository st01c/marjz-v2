### Short bio (tl;dr)

I am a researcher, curator and creative practitioner currently living and working between Ghent/Belgium and Dundee/Scotland. In Ghent, I work as [Senior Research Fellow](https://orpheusinstituut.be/nl/orpheus-research-centre/onderzoekers/martin-zeilinger) in the Posthuman Music ERC Advanced Grant project, hosted at the [Orpheus Institute for Advanced Studies and Research in Music](https://orpheusinstituut.be/en). In Dundee, I'm a [Reader in Computational Arts & Technology](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/persons/martin-zeilinger) at Abertay University (currently in a part-time role). My interests are many, and unresolved – they include artistic experimentation with emerging technologies (such as AI and blockchain); low-tech and analog counterpoints to computation; permacomputing; posthumanism and distributed agency; intellectual property issues in digital culture; aspects of experimental videogame culture; and critical cycling studies.

About a year before the generative AI and LLM hype really took off, I published [Tactical Entanglements](https://meson.press/books/tactical-entanglements/) (meson press, 2021, Open Access), a monograph that explores critical issues at the intersection of AI art, creative agency, posthumanism, copyright theory, and the digital commons. I'm also the author of [Structures of Belonging](https://aksioma.org/structures-of-belonging) (Aksioma Postscriptum Series, 2023), a shorter text that re-imagines blockchain technologies beyond digital property enclosures. Past main curatorial projects have included [Vector Festival](http://vectorfestival.org/) in Toronto/CAN (from 2014–2020) and the 2018 MoneyLab symposium in London/UK. My writing appears in books such as *[Artists Re:Thinking the Blockchain](https://torquetorque.net/wp-content/uploads/ArtistsReThinkingTheBlockchain.pdf)* and the *[MoneyLab Reader 2](https://networkcultures.org/blog/publication/moneylab-reader-2-overcoming-the-hype/)*, in journals including *Leonardo*, *Philosophy & Technology*, *Culture Machine*, and *Media Theory*, and in art magazines such as *Spike Art Magazine* and *Outland*. Since 2024, I have been co-organising [Wet Tech](https://marjz.notion.site/wet-tech), an experimental sound and moving-image art event in Dundee/UK.

### Longer bio

After many years in Vienna, Toronto, and London, I am living and working between Ghent/Belgium, where I am [Senior Research Fellow](<>) in the Posthuman Music ERC Advanced Grant project (hosted at the [Orpheus Institute for Advanced Studies and Research in Music](<>)), and Dundee/Scotland, where I work as [Reader in Computational Arts & Technology](https://rke.abertay.ac.uk/en/persons/martin-zeilinger) at Abertay University (currently in a part-time role). My interests are wide-ranging, and unseresolved. My research involves the theorisation of artistic and activist experiments with emerging technologies; digital art; posthumanism and distribuged agency; appropriation-based creative practices; cultural ownership and IP issues; political economies of new media; and experimental videogame cultures. A new focus area addresses issues of sustainability in relation to emerging technologies, for example through the concept of permacomputing.

A common thread in my work is how concepts like authorship, creativity, originality, agency, and ownership are reconfigured in emerging tech contexts, how they become legally or algorithmically encoded in service of capital, and how artist/activist communities respond critically to such developments. My monograph [Tactical Entanglements: AI Art, Creative Agency, and the Limits of Intellectual Property](https://meson.press/books/tactical-entanglements/) (meson press, 2021) pulls many of these concerns together. Recent contributions to critical perspectives on AI, posthumanist creativity, and non-human agency include essays in [Vectoral Agents: Power in the Age of Planetary Computation](https://networkcultures.org/blog/publication/vectoral-agents-power-in-the-age-of-planetary-computation/) (2025), [Decentralised Music: Exploring Blockchain for Artistic Research](https://orpheusinstituut.be/en/publications/decentralised-music) (2024), and [Chimeras: Inventory of Synthetic Cognition](https://www.onassis.org/culture/publications/chimeras-inventory-of-synthetic-cognition) (Onassis Foundation, 2022). My critical writing on blockchain technology also includes [Structures of Belonging](https://aksioma.org/structures-of-belonging) (Aksioma Postscriptum Series, 2023).

My writing has been translated into languages including Korean, French, Italian, Slovak, and Slovenian. It has appeared in in key journals such as *Leonardo*, *Philosophy & Technology*, *Computer Music Journal, Media Theory*, *Culture Machine*, and *Afterimage*. It has also been included in books such as [](https://www.distanz.de/en/metaspace-visions-of-space-from-the-middle-ages-to-the-digital-age)the [MoneyLab Reader Vol. 2](https://networkcultures.org/blog/publication/moneylab-reader-2-overcoming-the-hype/) (Institute of Network Cultures, 2018), [Artists Re:Thinking the Blockchain](https://torquetorque.net/wp-content/uploads/ArtistsReThinkingTheBlockchain.pdf) (Torque Editions, 2017), [Sampling Media](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/sampling-media-9780199949335)[ (Oxford University Press, 2014), and *Video Game Art Reader Vol. 2*.](https://marjz.net/item.html?id=survival-interventions-in-gta-the-limits-of-performance-in-virtual-environments) Art criticism has also been featured in publications including *Spike Art Magazine* and *Outland*.

I have presented at international art organisations and festivals focused on new media and technology (for example Serpentine Galleries, Aksioma, BEK, Uroboros Festival, Zentrum für Netzkunst, among others). My curatorial practice moves between conceptual, sound-based, and moving-image work, and frequently connects to academic contexts. I co-directed/co-curated Vector Festival in Toronto/CAN from 2014–2020, and have since then been involved in various curatorial projects in Europe, including [Rethinking Affordance](https://schloss-post.com/category/issues/rethinking-affordance/) (2018, co-curated with Ashley Scarlett). Since 2024, I am co-organising [Wet Tech](https://marjz.notion.site/wet-tech), an experimental sound and moving-image art event series in Dundee/UK (currently co-organised with Johanna Linsley and Jan Mertens). I occassionally also advise artists, filmmakers, and creative practitioners on IP questions (often around fair dealing/fair use questions) and have served as consultant and expert witness on topical legal cases.

My academic training includes a SSHRC Banting Postdoc with the legal anthropologist Prof. Rosemary Coombe (York Univ., Toronto/CAN, 2011-2013), an MA and PhD ("Arts and Politics of Appropriation") in Comparative Literature (Univ. of Toronto, 2003-2009), and wide-ranging (if incomplete) undergraduate work at the University of Vienna and the University of Applied Arts (also Vienna), in Cultural Studies, Comp. Lit., Philosophy, Media Theory, and Experimental Film.

I am a passionate cyclist, and since roughly 2023 have been involved in developing humanities-informed perspectives on "critical cycling studies" with Andrew B. Bricker. This has culminated in the publication of a [position paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05974-7) in *Humanities and Social Sciences* Commmunications (Springer Nature portfolio) in 2025.
